# Legal Tech Recruiter Signal Map

**Purpose:** translate the architecture portfolio into language currently used by legal-tech employers and buyers.

## Market signals observed in 2026

Current legal-tech roles and product literature repeatedly emphasize:

- AI workflow implementation;
- customer/legal-team enablement;
- solution design around real legal workflows;
- product feedback loops between users and engineering;
- legal research and document-analysis workflows;
- enterprise adoption and change management;
- measurable utilization and value;
- governance, confidentiality, privilege, and professional oversight;
- integrations into tools lawyers already use;
- source-grounded and evidence-backed outputs.

Examples include Harvey's Legal Engineer / Product Specialist and Customer Success roles, and Thomson Reuters' Solutions Engineering work around CoCounsel.

## Strongest positioning for this portfolio

### Primary narrative

> **AI systems architect focused on governed workflow transformation in high-stakes domains, with hands-on legal-tech architecture spanning evidence provenance, matter intelligence, human authorization, counsel handoff, and verified resolution.**

This is stronger than:
- "prompt engineer";
- "AI enthusiast";
- "legal chatbot builder";
- "generalist consultant."

### Secondary narrative

> **I translate messy operational workflows into source-grounded AI systems with explicit state, authority, approvals, integrations, and auditability.**

That language travels well across legal tech, ed tech, fintech, insurance, and other regulated domains.

### Roles that may require legal credentials
Treat these separately because many employers explicitly require practicing-lawyer experience:
- Legal Engineer at vendors where the role is defined as lawyer-facing substantive legal expertise;
- attorney product specialist;
- legal research attorney;
- legal innovation counsel.

The portfolio can still support adjacent solution, implementation, enablement, product, governance, and architecture roles without representing legal credentials the candidate does not hold.

## What to show, not merely say

Recruiter credibility increases when architecture claims have visible artifacts.

Priority public artifacts:
- architecture diagrams;
- typed object schemas;
- sanitized workflow examples;
- synthetic matter walkthrough;
- before/after workflow map;
- implementation-status labels;
- sample audit receipt;
- sample Principal Brief;
- synthetic adversarial analysis;
- API/data-contract snippets that expose no proprietary implementation;
- screenshots of working interfaces where available.
